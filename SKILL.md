# Equity Due Diligence

Find the strongest **conditional** stock candidate for the user's market, time horizon, risk capacity, currency, and constraints. Treat "the best" as a comparison under stated assumptions, never as a certain future return. Investigate overlooked details in lawful public information; do not seek, solicit, or use material nonpublic information.

## Set the decision frame

- For this user's default workflow, search instruments available through the Baraka investing app. Verify the current asset listing, account jurisdiction, costs, and tradability in Baraka before naming a candidate as actionable; its catalog and terms can change. The user has no preferred exchange. Keep other markets in scope when Baraka offers them.
- Establish investment horizon, risk or loss capacity, base currency, and relevant constraints (such as income, sector, or Sharia requirements). Ask only for missing inputs that would change the conclusion. If the user delegates these choices, use a conservative long-term comparison as an explicit research assumption; do not infer unlimited loss tolerance or personalize a trade without the needed facts.
- Distinguish a request to **research** from a request to **trade**. Research authorization alone does not authorize opening accounts, placing orders, or changing a portfolio.
- Choose a defensible comparison set and disclose how candidates entered it. Include a diversified benchmark or a cash/no-purchase outcome when it meaningfully tests whether a single stock is worthwhile.

## Gather and test evidence

1. Browse current sources on every run. Confirm the issuer's legal name, ticker, listing, share class, and reporting currency. Prefer regulator or exchange filings and issuer investor-relations documents for company facts. Use current market data with an explicit quote time and delay status. Consult [official sources](references/official-sources.md) for starting points, then verify that the relevant links and rules still work.
2. Read the latest annual report, latest interim report, and subsequent material announcements; inspect earlier periods when a trend or restatement matters. Record fiscal period end, filing/publication date, retrieval date, units, and whether figures are audited. Do not present an old filing or quote as current.
3. Build an evidence ledger for every decision-driving claim: claim or number; exact document/link and page, section, or filing identifier; period and publication date; units/currency; calculation if derived; status as verified, disputed, estimated, or unavailable. Reconcile material conflicts before ranking. Separate reported facts, management guidance, market consensus, and your own inference.
4. Compare business durability, growth quality, cash generation, balance-sheet and refinancing risk, valuation, governance, catalysts, and investor fit. Use sector-appropriate measures; banks, insurers, REITs, and loss-making firms need different metrics. Read [diligence method](references/diligence-method.md) when screening, valuing, or investigating a candidate.
5. Challenge the apparent winner. Look for evidence against the thesis in notes, audit opinions, related-party transactions, dilution, cash-flow conversion, customer concentration, contingent liabilities, and later disclosures. Distinguish a real anomaly from an accounting or seasonal explanation. Never assert wrongdoing from an unexplained pattern alone.

## Decide and report

- Show the candidate universe, selection criteria, and a compact comparison. Explain the leading **conditional** choice, the strongest alternative, why each could fail, and what new evidence would reverse the ranking. Say "insufficient evidence" or "no attractive candidate" when warranted; a composite score must not force a winner.
- Give base, optimistic, and adverse scenarios with explicit assumptions and a plausible valuation range or break-even conditions, rather than a single precise target. Account for dilution, debt, currency, taxes/fees when relevant, and possible permanent loss. Distinguish forecast from fact.
- Put the as-of timestamp and source links close to the claims they support. State quote delay, unavailable data, confidence limits, and any material disagreement among sources. Make the evidence ledger available in the answer or a requested artifact so another person can reproduce the conclusion.
- Reply in the user's language. Explain what information would make the conclusion more tailored without holding up a useful conditional analysis.
