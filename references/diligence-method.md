# Stock research method

Use judgment and sector context. These are prompts for investigation, not a universal scoring formula.

## Comparable set and time basis

- Explain the candidate universe, exclusions, liquidity threshold if applied, and why peers are comparable. Avoid selecting only recent winners.
- Align fiscal periods, accounting standards, share counts, currencies, and enterprise-versus-equity value. Check splits, special dividends, buybacks, issuance, and one-time acquisitions before comparing per-share trends.
- For cyclical businesses, examine a full cycle where available. For a new or loss-making issuer, emphasize funding runway, dilution, unit economics, and uncertainty instead of forcing earnings multiples.

## Business and financial quality

- Identify revenue drivers, customer and supplier concentration, competitive position, regulation, and the economic mechanism behind expected growth.
- Compare revenue and operating-profit trends with operating cash flow and free cash flow. Explain working-capital swings and capital expenditure needs. Inspect receivables, inventory, deferred revenue, and nonrecurring adjustments when they diverge from reported earnings.
- Evaluate debt maturities, interest coverage, covenant exposure, leases, pension or other commitments, and refinancing under a weaker earnings or higher-rate case.
- Read auditor opinion and notes for restatements, related-party transactions, contingent claims, tax exposure, segment changes, and accounting-policy changes. Check insider and major-holder disclosures where available; trades alone do not establish a motive.

## Price, scenarios, and falsification

- Use a valuation approach that fits the business: normalized earnings or cash flow, enterprise value against operating output, dividend capacity, asset value, or a transparent discounted-cash-flow model. Compare with suitable peers and the company's own history only after explaining differences in growth, margins, leverage, and accounting.
- Write down the assumptions that drive value: revenue growth, margin, reinvestment, financing cost, dilution, terminal conditions, and currency. Use adverse, base, and optimistic cases. Show a simple sensitivity or break-even threshold when a small change reverses the conclusion.
- For each candidate, state the thesis, what observable evidence would disprove it, near-term catalysts, a plausible permanent-loss path, and why the current price does or does not compensate for that risk.
- Prefer a supported range and qualitative confidence over a false-precision price target or numerical probability without a defensible model.

## Evidence ledger shape

| Claim or metric | Exact source / location | Fiscal period | Published / retrieved | Currency and units | Calculation or reconciliation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use direct document links and page, section, or accession identifiers when possible. Mark status `verified`, `disputed`, `estimated`, or `unavailable`. Keep conflicting values visible until resolved; do not silently choose the convenient one.
